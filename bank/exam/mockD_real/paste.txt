What condition would best support Francis' trade proposal between AUD and ZAR?
Correct answer:

A. Low market volatility
Correct Answer Feedback: Correct because the payoff in a carry trade is the gradual accrual of the interest rate differential—income that is unrelated to exchange rate volatility. During periods of low turbulence, investors may feel relatively confident that exchange rate movements will not jeopardize the gradual accrual of the interest differential. When volatility in the currency markets spikes, however, the risk of an adverse exchange rate movement rises sharply relative to the gradual flow of income. During periods of low volatility, carry trades tend to generate positive returns, but they are prone to significant crash risk in turbulent times.

B. Covered interest rate parity not holding in the short term
Incorrect Answer Feedback: Incorrect because for a carry trade to succeed, it is uncovered interest rate parity, not covered interest rate parity, that must not hold. If it held, the investor would receive identical returns from either an unhedged foreign currency return or a domestic currency investment as the appreciation or depreciation of the exchange rate would offset the yield differential. If uncovered interest rate parity held at all times, investors would not be able to profit from a strategy that undertook long positions in high-yield currencies and short positions in low-yield currencies. The change in spot rates over the tenor of the forward contracts would cancel out the interest rate differentials locked in at the inception of the position.

Incorrect Answer Feedback: Incorrect because the difference in yields is locked in at the time of the trade, so the movement of the yield spread once the trade is initiated is irrelevant.
