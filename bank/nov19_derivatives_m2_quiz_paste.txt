Incorrect Answer Feedback: Incorrect because for a call option, the term e-rTXN(d2) is the bond component of the model. The BSM model can be described as having two components: a stock component and a bond component. For call options, the stock component is SN(d1) and the bond component is e–rTXN(d2).
Incorrect answer:

bought shares of a zero-coupon bond trading at e–rTX.
Incorrect Answer Feedback: Incorrect because this is the (negative) interpretation of a put option, not a call option. Note for call options, –N(d2) implies borrowing money or short selling N(d2) shares of a zero-coupon bond trading at e–rTX. For put options, N(–d2) implies lending money or buying N(–d2) shares of a zero-coupon bond trading at e–rTX.
Correct answer:

the probability that the call option expires in the money.
Correct Answer Feedback: Correct because the N(d2) term has an important interpretation. It is a unique measure of the probability that the call option expires in the money.
